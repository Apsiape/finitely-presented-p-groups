# Finitely presented infinite p-groups for every prime

Seth Douglas and Nidhal Mghirbi

<!-- DOI badge and version line go here at mint. -->

For every prime p there is an infinite, residually finite group with an ordinary finite presentation in which
every element has finite p-power order. For p = 2 this is a recent construction of OpenAI, which also answered the
finitely presented Burnside question. This paper extends it to every prime, from a single finite presentation
written over the integers.

## What the paper proves

- **Theorem A (nil algebras in every positive characteristic).** One finite presentation by word equalities
  u = v and word zeros w = 0, fixed in advance. Over every field K of positive characteristic, the nonunital
  algebra I_K it defines is finitely presented, infinite-dimensional and not nilpotent, and every square matrix
  over I_K is nilpotent. So I_K is Jacobson radical, and K + I_K is a finitely presented, algebraic,
  infinite-dimensional unital algebra. For K = F_2 this is OpenAI's theorem.
- **Theorem B (finitely presented p-groups for every prime).** Let R_p be the unital algebra of the same
  presentation over F_p. Then Gamma_p = St_12(R_p) is infinite, ordinarily finitely presented, periodic and
  residually finite, and G_p = ker(Gamma_p -> St_12(F_p)) is an infinite, ordinarily finitely presented,
  residually finite group in which every element has finite p-power order. Both groups have Kazhdan's property
  (T), and the element orders of G_p are unbounded. For p = 2 these are OpenAI's groups.
- **Theorem C (characteristic zero needs signs).** Over a field of characteristic zero, an algebra generated by
  finitely many letters subject only to word equalities and word zeros is nil only if it is nilpotent. The
  statement is elementary, and the paper does not claim it as new; it locates exactly where the
  positive-characteristic mechanism lives.

## Why odd primes are not a relabelling

- **Cancellation.** The characteristic-two symmetric sum of d x d matrices vanishes once m > d^2. In
  characteristic p the bound must depend on p. The paper uses m > (p - 1) d^2, which has a two-line proof;
  Zalesskii's theorem gives the sharp degree dp. The hierarchy absorbs the extra factor (Section 3).
- **Homological stability.** The unordered-frame stability argument fails over odd fields: an explicit stabilizer
  element has a non-elementary complementary block (Proposition 6.5). It is replaced by an integral ordered-frame
  argument whose one face comparison uses a signed transposition (Section 6).
- **Signs in the group calculations.** Swaps become signed transpositions; permutations of coordinates carry a
  compensating sign on an unused coordinate (Lemma 7.5); orders of new roots come from C^p = [U^p, V] (Sections 5-7).
- **Phases.** Central elements are detected with long-word phases modulo integers prime to p. The phases are
  reduced modulo the part of L_0 ... L_d prime to p, which is unbounded by the lifting-the-exponent lemma, so one
  presentation serves every prime (Section 8).

## Building the paper

Run `pdflatex main.tex` three times in `paper/` (packages amsmath, amssymb, amsthm, mathtools, enumitem, geometry,
microtype and hyperref).

## The checks

Run `python verify/checks.py` (Python 3 with numpy). It checks, in integer arithmetic modulo p: the symmetric-sum
bound of Lemma 3.1 and Zalesskii's degree dp, exhaustively on tuples of matrix units for small p and d, and that the
characteristic-two bound fails over F_3; the matrices of Proposition 6.5 and of the face comparison in Theorem 6.8; the coordinate changes
of Section 7; and the valuation bound of Lemma 8.6. The checks support the proofs; they do not replace them.

## Credits

The construction and the principal methods are OpenAI's ("An infinite finitely presented periodic group",
23 September 2026; "An infinite finitely presented residually finite 2-group", 5 October 2026; both in
[openai/math](https://github.com/openai/math)): the finite rule hierarchy, the matrix-weighted cancellation, the
coefficient extraction, the finite presentation of Steinberg groups, root strips, centrality and stability, the
shift substitutions, and the long-word phases and detection. What is new here is the extension to every prime and
the repairs it needs. Section 10 of the paper says how the work was done and verified, including the use of AI
models.
